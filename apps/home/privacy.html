Privacy

sawt and its nine apps are made and run by Amer Harb. You can play every app without an account. Signing in is optional, and it keeps as little as it can. There are no ads, no tracking cookies, and nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

Last updated 12 October 2026.

Playing, without signing in

  • Your settings stay on your device — the language, the theme, the items you hid, your courtyard animal. They live in your browser's storage, and so do the recordings an app keeps for playing offline. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes all of it.
  • Anonymous statistics. The apps tell our collector, sada, when an app is opened, how a finished round went (which items it had, which were found, how long it took, which wrong answers were tried) and which languages are chosen. None of it carries a name, an account, a cookie or any other identifier. The country is worked out from your internet address with DB-IP's country database, and the address itself is then dropped, never stored. We keep these to see which apps are used and where they are hard.
  • The courtyard 🏟️ — playing together in a room — goes through our server saha. It holds a room's code, its players' animals and the moves only in memory, while the room is open, and stores nothing: no names, no free text, no database.
  • Visit counts. The pages use Vercel Web Analytics, which counts page views without cookies and without following anyone from one site, or one day, to the next.

Feedback

When you send feedback from an app (💬), sada keeps what you wrote, the app and the kind of feedback, any details you filled in — a language, a sound, items, a link — and the country, worked out as above. It keeps an email address only if you typed one, so we can write back. Feedback is not tied to an account. To have something you sent deleted, ask through the feedback sheet, as below, and say roughly when you sent it.

Signing in

Signing in lets an app's settings follow you to another device. It goes through our sign-in service, baab, which keeps:

  • Your email address, as a keyed hash — enough to recognise the same address the next time, but it cannot be turned back into the address. The address itself is not stored.
  • Your account: the handle baab makes for it (like K7Q4-X2M9), a nickname if you set one, the settings of each app you play while signed in, and the date the account was made.
  • Your sign-ins. Signing in on a device lasts 180 days or until you sign out. It is held in one cookie, sawt_session, set only when you sign in and readable only by baab — the one cookie sawt uses. Sign-in codes and links work for 10 minutes and 5 tries, and are stored only as hashes. A sign-in that has run out can no longer be used; its record stays until the account is deleted.

To send your sign-in code, baab hands your address to Resend, our email service, for that one mail; Resend keeps its own record of mail it delivers. baab does not record your internet address.

Deleting your account

Signed in, open the sign-in sheet and choose Delete my account. It deletes the handle, the nickname, every app's settings and every sign-in, at once and for good. Each device keeps its own copy of its settings until you clear the site's data there. The anonymous statistics and any feedback were never tied to the account, so they are not part of it.

Children

The apps are played by children as well as by adults, and at any age playing needs no account and no personal details. Signing in asks for an email address: if you are under 13, ask a parent or guardian to sign in for you.

Your rights

You can ask what we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, or object to how it is used. Most of it you can do yourself — the nickname in the sign-in sheet, the account with its delete button, the device's copy by clearing site data. For anything else, or a question, write to us through the feedback sheet in any app — ⚙️, then 👤, then 💬 — and add your email address if you would like an answer. If you think we have handled your data wrongly, you can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY).
